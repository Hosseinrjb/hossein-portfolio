# Captive Insurance Feasibility Study

**Research synthesis and financial scenarios · Risk & Insurance Studies Centre / HAIIvVE 9 · January to May 2025**

[Portfolio](../README.md)

## Overview and problem

This team study examines whether a Cayman Islands captive-insurance subsidiary could help CIBC manage selected risks and long-term insurance costs. We reviewed industry research, estimated costs, and considered governance requirements before recommending a detailed feasibility study.

## Research sources and tools

The materials draw on public industry publications, captive-insurance research, and cited fee schedules and regulatory documents. The white paper includes a multi-page bibliography; some references remain unresolved.

Methods: Public-source research, comparative analysis, and financial scenario modelling. I list Excel modelling in my résumé, but the workbook is not included. Its formulas and scenario calculations therefore cannot be checked here.

## Methodology

1. Explain captive structures and compare them with conventional insurance.
2. Assess the Cayman Islands as a potential domicile using the sources available during the study.
3. Summarize governance and Canadian/Cayman regulatory considerations as understood in the original paper.
4. Estimate setup costs, annual operating costs, and potential capital needs separately.
5. Recommend detailed feasibility work using internal loss, premium, and exposure data, actuarial analysis, and specialist review.

The team recommended a detailed feasibility study, rather than treating these estimates as sufficient for a launch decision. Savings described in the paper are prospective and have not been demonstrated using internal CIBC loss or premium data.

## Key insights

- Setup and operating expenses must be distinguished from the capital needed to absorb losses.
- Domicile selection alone cannot establish viability; the retained risks, reinsurance, governance, and capital treatment matter.
- A full business case would require internal exposure data and adverse-loss scenarios, which are not provided here.
