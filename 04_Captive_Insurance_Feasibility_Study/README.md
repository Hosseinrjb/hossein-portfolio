# Captive Insurance Feasibility Study

**Research synthesis and financial scenarios · Risk & Insurance Studies Centre / HAIIvVE 9 · January–May 2025**

[Portfolio](../README.md) · [White paper (PDF, 24 pages)](White_Paper_CIBC.pdf) · [Infographic (PDF, 3 pages)](HAIIVVE9_Infographic_Team7.pdf)

## Overview and problem

This team study examines whether a Cayman Islands captive-insurance subsidiary could help CIBC manage selected risks and long-term insurance costs. It connects industry research, preliminary cost scenarios, and governance considerations to a recommendation for further analysis.

**Availability:** A documentation-only case study with a white paper and infographic. No internal CIBC data, actuarial model, source dataset, or Excel workbook is supplied. This is a student research study, not evidence of an implemented CIBC captive or realized savings.

## Research sources and tools

The materials draw on public industry publications, captive-insurance research, and cited fee schedules and regulatory documents. The white paper includes a multi-page bibliography; individual references have not all been authenticated.

**Methods:** Public-source research, comparative analysis, and financial scenario modeling. The résumé identifies Excel as the modeling tool, but the workbook is absent, so formulas and scenario calculations cannot be independently inspected.

## Methodology

1. Explain captive structures and compare them with conventional insurance.
2. Assess the Cayman Islands as a potential domicile using the sources available during the study.
3. Summarize governance and Canadian/Cayman regulatory considerations as understood in the original paper.
4. Estimate setup costs, annual operating costs, and potential capital needs separately.
5. Recommend detailed feasibility work using internal loss, premium, and exposure data, actuarial analysis, and specialist review.

## Findings and estimates

The following are **study assumptions and estimates**, not observed CIBC expenditures or current quotations.

| Scenario component | Estimate in the source materials | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial setup | USD 155,366 | Preliminary setup-cost table in white paper §6.2 |
| Annual operating costs | USD 165,366 in the modeled first year, 2026 | Projected operating costs, excluding economic capital |
| Annual inflation | 2.5% | Assumption for subsequent operating-cost projections |
| Economic/operating capital | USD 1–10 million or more | Broad range requiring risk-specific actuarial analysis |

The team recommended a **detailed feasibility study**, rather than treating these estimates as sufficient for a launch decision. Savings described in the paper are prospective and have not been demonstrated using internal CIBC loss or premium data.

## Key insights

- Setup and operating expenses must be distinguished from the capital needed to absorb losses.
- Domicile selection alone cannot establish viability; the retained risks, reinsurance, governance, and capital treatment matter.
- A decision-ready business case requires internal exposure data and adverse-loss scenarios, which are not provided here.

## Limitations and review items

- Reliance on public benchmarks limits organization-specific cost and savings estimates.
- No workbook is included to verify calculations or sensitivity analyses.
- Bibliography entries include explicit placeholders such as “Illustrative Source,” “article pages pending,” and “generic reference, check source if needed,” plus inconsistent citation metadata. These need source-by-source review before relying on the paper's conclusions.
- Regulatory, tax, and capital statements are historical research claims from the paper and have not been validated as current guidance. The README makes no independent claim that a particular structure qualifies for regulatory capital relief.
- The résumé reports 40+ sources and first place among seven teams. The supplied PDFs do not independently establish the source-count criterion or competition ranking; these claims are reserved for owner verification.
